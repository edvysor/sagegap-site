The gap between systems

The institutions can work, and the family can still lose the thread.

Schools, providers, therapists, evaluators, and families can each hold valid pieces of a child's story. The difficulty often comes when information needs to move between them. Important details get lost, and families have to fill in the gaps.

A family crisis should not become an information crisis.

01

The school has one file. A provider has another. A therapist has a third. Families are expected to connect what the systems do not.

02

A new teacher, building, district, or caregiver can force a child to restart the story instead of continuing it.

03

Families receive dense documents, unfamiliar terms, and consequential decisions on institutional timelines.

04

Without a record you can look back on, it can be hard to remember what worked, what was promised, and what happened next.

What is the Canonical Egg?

The Canonical Egg is SageGap’s name for the proposed family-held record: a connected history that keeps source documents identifiable while helping your family organize them across years and services. It does not replace the school’s or provider’s original records.

How would SageGap create these explanations?

The technology: SageGap is being designed to use superintelligence to help prepare optional, plain-language explanations and suggestions. These would be clearly labeled, kept separate from the original documents, and available for families to review.

The limits: An automatically generated explanation can make mistakes. It should be checked against the school-issued or provider-issued record and reviewed by a person, especially before important decisions.

Your role: Software does not become the authority over your child’s information. Your family chooses what to keep and share; original record issuers remain responsible for their own records.

These are planned safeguards, not a claim that live record interpretation or sharing is available today.
