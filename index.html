Why SageGap Exists

There is a gap between
what families know
and what they have a right to.

01

IEP meetings are not designed for families. They are designed for compliance. Parents are handed documents they've never seen and asked to sign at the end of a 45-minute meeting.

02

A child's records do not follow them the way they should. Every school transition, every new teacher, starts from scratch. The family holds the history — but rarely the tools to use it.

03

Teachers want to differentiate. Systems don't give them time. The gap between what a child needs and what an overloaded teacher can build costs real learning, every day.

04

Multilingual families navigate this in a second language. The complexity of special education law lands on families already carrying the weight of cultural and linguistic distance.

"Families don't need to be fixed.
They need to be
equipped."

SageGap was built from the inside — by someone who spent nearly two decades in Title I inclusion classrooms, who navigated IEP processes as a parent, and who knows that the families showing up to those meetings love their children fiercely and deserve a system that honors that.

The Canonical Egg

Most platforms store your child's data for institutional convenience. The Canonical Egg is different — it's a longitudinal, family-owned record of your child's learning journey that travels with them, grows with them, and belongs to no one but you.
