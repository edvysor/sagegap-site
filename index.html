The gap between systems

The institutions can work, and the family can still lose the thread.

Schools, providers, therapists, evaluators, and families can each hold valid pieces of a child's story. The failure happens in the handoffs: information gets separated, context disappears, and the family becomes the only bridge.

A family crisis should not become an information crisis.

01

Records live in different places.

The school has one file. A provider has another. A therapist has a third. Families are expected to connect what the systems do not.

02

Transitions erase context.

A new teacher, building, district, or caregiver can force a child to restart the story instead of continuing it.

03

Meetings move faster than understanding.

Families receive dense documents, unfamiliar terms, and consequential decisions on institutional timelines.

04

Advocacy depends on memory.

Without a longitudinal record, patterns, promises, and prior supports are easy to miss precisely when they matter most.

A real family moment

The information existed. Access did not.

“When their mom was out of town, I needed medical paperwork and school information for our children. I knew the information existed, but I just couldn’t get to all of it quickly. Pieces lived in different portals, folders, messages, and with different people. Meanwhile, the school and doctor still needed answers. Having one family-controlled place for the current, verified records would have turned a stressful search into a simple handoff.”

A dad of two school-age childrenOne of the real-life moments behind SageGap

Trust by design

Your data. Your rules. The family remains in the loop.

SageGap is being designed around a responsibility stack as seriously as its product stack: privacy, permission, explainability, source traceability, and human confirmation are product requirements, not footer language.

The operating principle is simple: intelligent systems may organize, compare, or suggest. They should not silently become the authority over a child's story.

✓You control what enters

Family confirmation is required before information becomes part of the trusted record.

✓You control who sees it

Access is intentional, limited, and revocable.

✓Your data is not the product

The value proposition is clarity and continuity, not selling a child's information.

✓The record survives transitions

The family retains continuity when institutions, staff, or locations change.
